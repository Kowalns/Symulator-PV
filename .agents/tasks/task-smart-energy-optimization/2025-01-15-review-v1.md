# Smart energy optimization: price-driven heat pump scheduling and grid-fallback battery charging

This change adds two price-optimization features to the PV simulator's economic analysis. First, the heat pump can now concentrate its electrical load into the cheapest RCE hours of the day instead of spreading evenly, controlled by a new `moc_pompy_ciepla_kw` parameter. Second, the battery can charge from the grid as a fallback when PV is insufficient, using a two-pass daily algorithm that plans grid charging in the cheapest hours while tracking energy provenance (PV vs grid) to enforce the rule that grid-sourced energy cannot be sold. Both optimizations activate only for dynamic tariffs (`G11f_dynamiczna`, `G11_dynamiczna`); G11 behavior is unchanged.

Watch for: the autokonsumpcja peak-discharge path is a no-op that leaves grid-sourced energy stranded in the battery (confirmed); pass-1 planning overestimates available capacity because it ignores the peak-hour discharge event (likely); grid charging can double-charge in the same hour as PV surplus if both conditions are met (confirmed).

**Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

The heat pump optimization in `energy_profile.py` is a slot-packing algorithm: compute hours needed from daily demand and pump power, pick the cheapest RCE hours for the month, distribute uniformly. The same hour set is reused for every day within a month — a valid simplification given that `pobierz_cene_rce` returns monthly averages, not daily spot prices.

The battery logic in `economics.py` uses a two-pass daily algorithm. Pass 1 simulates PV charging on temporary state to determine remaining capacity; the planner selects the cheapest hours to fill the gap from grid. Pass 2 executes the actual simulation with both PV and grid charging applied. Energy provenance is tracked with two separate accumulators (`magazyn_stan_pv`, `magazyn_stan_siec`), and discharge reduces both proportionally. The sell path only drains the PV bucket, preventing grid-energy arbitrage.

The peak-hour discharge for autokonsumpcja mode has a gap: the explicit peak-hour block is a `pass` statement, relying entirely on the general deficit-discharge path to drain the battery. If there's no deficit in the peak hour (PV still producing at 16:00 in summer), no discharge happens and the battery sits idle with grid energy that was bought specifically for evening use.

## Two-pass daily algorithm and pass-1 fidelity

Pass 1 models PV surplus charging and deficit autokonsumpcja discharge on `tmp_pv` / `tmp_siec` copies. It does not model the peak-hour discharge (sprzedaz or forced autokonsumpcja) that reduces battery state before end-of-day.

For `priorytet == "sprzedaz"`, the peak-hour discharge in pass 2 drains `magazyn_stan_pv`. If pass 1 doesn't account for this drain, `magazyn_stan_po_pv` overestimates end-of-day state, causing `brakujaca_energia` to be underestimated. The battery schedules fewer grid-charge hours than it should, and ends the day below full capacity. Over a year this compounds into reduced self-consumption savings.

A fix: simulate the peak-hour discharge in pass 1, or compute `brakujaca_energia` from `magazyn_pojemnosc_wh` directly (always targeting full battery regardless of intra-day drains).

## Grid-PV double-charge collision

In pass 2, the execution order within an hour is:

```
1. PV surplus → charge magazyn_stan_pv
2. Grid fallback → charge magazyn_stan_siec (if g in godziny_ladowania_siec)
3. Peak discharge (if g == godzina_rozladowania)
```

If hour `g` has PV surplus AND is in `godziny_ladowania_siec`, step 1 fills some capacity, reducing what step 2 can deliver. In practice, PV hours tend to be midday (expensive RCE) and grid-charge hours tend to be nighttime (cheap RCE), so this collision is rare with representative price curves. But the code doesn't explicitly exclude hours with expected PV surplus from the grid-charge plan.

## Autokonsumpcja peak-discharge dead code

```python
elif magazyn.priorytet == "autokonsumpcja" and g == godzina_rozladowania:
    # Rozladuj caly magazyn na autokonsumpcje w szczycie
    # (juz obsluzone w sekcji niedoboru powyzej, ale jesli nie bylo
    #  niedoboru w tej godzinie - nie rozladowujemy dodatkowo)
    pass
```

The comment acknowledges the gap: if there's no deficit in the peak hour, nothing happens. The battery bought grid energy specifically to serve load during the expensive peak. If PV production covers the load at that hour, the grid energy stays trapped until a future hour has a deficit — but future hours might be cheaper than the peak, eroding the optimization's value.

The fix: force-discharge the battery to cover the hour's full load (displacing the PV surplus to be sold), or implement lookahead to pre-serve upcoming hours' demand. The simplest approach: in the peak hour, discharge the battery to offset `zuzycie` regardless of whether PV covers it — the displaced PV surplus then gets sold at the (high) peak-hour price, which is economically equivalent to buying cheap and selling expensive but with the battery as intermediary for self-produced energy.

## Energy source tracking asymmetry

After several days of grid fallback charging, the grid fraction of battery state grows. Any deficit discharge then pulls mostly from grid (proportional model), keeping the PV fraction elevated. For sprzedaz mode this means peak-hour sells remain high even as the battery becomes grid-dominated. This isn't a bug but an emergent consequence worth noting: FIFO vs proportional accounting would produce meaningfully different annual revenue figures.

## Test coverage gaps

Not tested: the autokonsumpcja peak-discharge no-op scenario (battery full, no deficit at peak hour); double-charge collision in same hour; multi-day battery state accumulation (tests use simple daily-reset patterns); behavior with negative RCE prices (the algorithm correctly picks those first since sort is ascending, but no test verifies the economic outcome).
